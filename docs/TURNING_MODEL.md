## Why a separate manoeuvring model

The prepared S175 constant matrices represent a seakeeping approximation.
Applying their equivalent horizontal damping to sustained rudder turning gave
about 1.76 degrees/minute, even with a physical propeller and rudder. Raising
PID gains cannot overcome a saturated rudder. Reducing only D66 would break
the coupled damping model; that earlier override remains removed.

The new implementation separates low-frequency (LF) manoeuvring from
wave-frequency (WF) motion. It does not force a yaw rate, turn radius, heading,
or speed. A requested 0.75 degrees/second is an order-of-magnitude comparison,
not an input to the equations. Speed can fall, the rudder takes time to move,
and the ship heels during a turn.

## Equations and ownership of forces

`game/simulation/maneuvering.py` contains the pure Son–Nomoto hull polynomials.
`game/simulation/two_timescale.py` contains the native four-DOF kinetics and
geometric composition. `game/simulation/runtime.py` advances LF, WF and actual
rudder/shaft states together on the fixed RK4 clock.

The authoritative LF state is `[u,v,p,r,N,E,phi,psi]`, relative to the water.
Rigid mass and roll/yaw inertias come from S175. Added masses and their
couplings use the original Son–Nomoto convention, in `[u,v,p,r]` order:

```
M00 = m + mx
M[v,p,r] = [[m+my, -my*ly, my*alpha],
            [-my*ly, Ixx+Jx, 0],
            [my*alpha, 0, Izz+Jz]]
Fc = [(m+my)*v*r, -(m+mx)*u*r, mx*lx*u*r, 0]
M * acceleration = hull + actuator + wind + mean_surge_drift + Fc - roll_restore
```

Here `mx'=0.000238`, `my'=0.007049`, `Jx'=0.0000034`, `Jz'=0.000419`,
`alpha/L=0.05`, and `lx/L=ly/L=0.0313`. Dimensional mass/inertia scales are
`rho*L^3/2` and `rho*L^5/2`. Roll restoring is `G44*phi`, applied once.
The prepared S175 damping, cross-flow and full added-mass Coriolis are not
added to these empirical LF equations. Doing so would double-count terms or
mix source conventions. LF kinematics retain the original `phi_dot=p` and
`psi_dot=cos(phi)*r`. Uniform current advects position once.

WF uses the original twelve-state `dynamics.derivative`, with unchanged S175
M, MA, D, G, full Coriolis and cross-flow, zero additional current, and only
the first-order oscillating wave force. Wave encounter is evaluated using LF
heading and water velocity. Mean surge drift belongs to LF. The data has no
validated sway/yaw mean-drift tables, so none are invented. Wind acts on LF
using the mean ground velocity. A calm WF state stays exactly zero during a
pure LF turn.

The rotations compose as `R = Rz(psi_LF) Rx(phi_LF) R_WF`, and position as
`[N,E,0] + R_LF * displacement_WF`. Velocities and accelerations include the
rotating-frame terms, including Coriolis, centripetal and angular-acceleration
contributions. Cargo and exposure use this composed total response. Native
LF `r` is not substituted for the actual geometric heading rate.

## Rudder, propeller and control

Actual loads use the original `container.m` propeller/rudder equations in SI,
including sway/yaw inflow and rudder roll moment. The original reproduction
retains its ±10 degree bound. The new full-rudder variant uses ±35 degree
orders, first-order servos, a 5 degrees/second rudder rate bound and 160 RPM
maximum ahead order. These are actuator constraints, not direct yaw caps.

The heading controller uses LF heading and actual LF heading rate. Its
source-style baseline has Kp=1 and Td=10 seconds, with a bounded integral
(Ti=300 seconds) enabled near the ordered heading. It commands rudder only.
Speed feedback uses source straight-hull resistance feedforward and maps a
force request into ahead RPM; it cannot hold speed by overwriting velocity.
The old teaching moment/thrust caps and reference yaw-rate limit do not apply
to this mode. Snapshots store both LF/WF states and actual/order servo states.

The UI `turn_rate` field is signed actual Euler heading rate, in degrees/s:
`(sin(roll)*q + cos(roll)*r) / cos(pitch)` converted from radians. Positive means
starboard. HELM and the temporary order popup display it. Historical replays
without body rates derive an estimate from unwrapped headings and timestamps;
a missing estimate displays a dash, not an invented zero.

## Reference conditions and limits

The [2021 self-propulsion study](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1312/9/9/1017)
uses a 1:42.8 S175 model, ±35 degree rudder and an initial 12.08 kn full-scale
speed. Table 6 scales to about 151–161 seconds to 90 degrees, 292–305 seconds
to 180 degrees, and tactical diameters of 752–767 m. It does not require a
90-degree turn in 120 seconds from initial rudder application.

The separate [2025 MMG study](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1312/13/8/1578)
reports open-water TD=4.51L and steady diameter=4.11L. Its model-scale
0.090 rad/s scales to about 0.679 degrees/s, and 35.27 seconds to 180 degrees
scales to about 268 seconds. Different loading, actuator coefficients and
procedures prevent treating both studies as one exact calibration target.
For L=175 m, IMO advance=4.5L and TD=5L give comparison limits of 787.5 and
875 m. Passing those two comparisons alone is not IMO certification.

This hybrid keeps S175 mass, inertias, restoring and original WF hydrodynamics,
but LF empirical derivatives and actuator interaction coefficients are reused
from another loading. Original Son–Nomoto draft/GM are 8.5 m/0.3 m; prepared
S175 values are 9.5 m/0.996 m. The 35-degree extension and 5-degree/s steering
gear need vessel-specific calibration. No coefficient is fitted merely to hit
0.75 degrees/s or pass a game mission.

The LF/WF split neglects slow-frame feedback into the WF equations and some
cross-frequency hydrodynamic interactions. It is an engineering two-time-scale
approximation, not a fully coupled CFD or validated real-vessel digital twin.
The actuator model supports ahead flow; reverse RPM and astern manoeuvring are
unsupported. Its low-speed inflow envelope `abs(r)*L/U <= 1.5` is a numerical
restriction, not a measured ship limit. Strong-wave, very-low-speed and extreme
heel behaviour require further experimental validation.

The four scenario budgets and gameplay fields are retained. Their old passing
reports and frozen replays describe earlier physics and cannot establish new
mission feasibility. New full-session strategy runs are required before those
missions can be republished as validated.
